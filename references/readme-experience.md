# Reader experience in persona READMEs

Read this when authoring or reviewing a generated persona README. Keep it out of the
generated persona's ordinary runtime routes. It is a short editorial step within the
existing production/completion pass, with no new source hunt, planning agent, evidence
schema or recognition round.

The four obligations are recognizable character, an imaginable interaction, a usable
first action and honest expectations. They apply to the whole page: orientation, uses
and source organization should continue the particularity of the opening. Shared content
responsibilities do not require a shared literary style. Conversation excerpts, simulated
multi-turn exchanges and sample persona responses are not README content elements or
necessary demonstrations of character. Use concrete situations, individual questions
and task statements without accompanying answers.

## Prepare from the supported perspective

Inspect the existing README, core, reconstruction scope at
`transworld-identity/scope.md`, frameworks, voice, relevant evidence modules and current
status records. Identify what draws this perspective's attention, what it asks the reader
to do, and what makes that interaction particular. Trace substantive character claims
through existing evidence links; do not duplicate the recognition profile. A narrow
record calls for a well-written narrow characterization, not invented biography.

Write the README in the default output language declared in the generated `SKILL.md`.
Apply this to headings, introductory prose, suggested prompts, usage, scope and status;
source titles and attributed quotations may retain their original language. Do not
infer the README language from the authoring conversation or a temporary request to
switch the persona's response language. Honor an explicit request for a different
README language.

Choose a realistic starting situation within the supported use. Include useful friction
where supported: asking for an example, resisting a comforting verdict, noticing an
overlooked constraint, or questioning the terms of the question. Friction is not
compulsory confrontation. Reserve, intensity, patience, humor and exuberance can each
convey presence. Do not impose a friendly tutor voice, generic warmth, catchphrases,
fabricated memories or endorsements. Use first person for the self-introduction;
the supporting documentation can use ordinary editorial narration.

## Default content order

Follow this order by default. Explicit user instructions and existing documentation
requirements of the destination repository take precedence. Adapt section titles,
paragraph rhythm and expression to the persona. Any reordering must keep the complete
opening visible, the usage entry easy to find and important limitations clear.

| Order | Content responsibility | Reader's question | Default presentation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title and persona introduction | What kind of person am I encountering? | A complete, uninterrupted first-person introduction directly below the title |
| 2 | One-sentence orientation and page navigation | What will this README help me understand? | A concise orientation sentence and navigation links in body-section order |
| 3 | What to think about together | What questions can I bring? | Concrete situations, individual questions or a task list; a small topic relationship map where useful |
| 4 | Getting started | How do I install, invoke and choose a language? | The shortest actionable steps, followed by necessary configuration guidance |
| 5 | Repository structure and loading relationships | What provides voice, reasoning, knowledge and maintenance? | A compact file diagram or table, with links to actual files |
| 6 | Sources and their contributions | How do these materials shape the perspective? | A contribution table organized by questions or concerns; a source map where relationships warrant one |
| 7 | Reconstruction scope and current status | What does this version support, and what remains unverified? | The reconstruction notice with the introduction's editorial-basis and birth-date source links, visible scope, actual status and supporting records |
| 8 | License | How may I use and share it? | A license link and exclusions for original sources and other material |

These are content responsibilities, not eight mandatory headings. There is no separate
conversation-excerpt section. Concrete situations and questions remain part of the page.

## Let the persona introduce itself

For a new persona or a requested introduction rewrite, put the complete first-person
self-introduction immediately below the README title, fully visible before any tagline,
badges, navigation, diagrams or intervening heading. Never collapse it in `<details>`.
The reader should encounter the particular person before the repository's construction.

At the first self-identification, write the subject's name followed by their birth date
in parentheses. Use the README's language and a clear date format; prefer a complete
day, month and year when supported. Do not include a death date or a birth–death range
in the title or opening. Check the date against supplied material or an already verified
biographical record and retain its locator in existing provenance. If only a year is
supported, use that precision; if the date is disputed or unknown, state that briefly
in the parentheses. Do not invent a birthday or expand source acquisition merely to
fill this field. A title containing the name and birth date can supply this identification
without requiring a duplicate in the prose.

Derive the voice from the actual core, voice module, frameworks and situated evidence:
what catches this person's attention, how they reframe a question, what they will risk
or refuse, and how they respond to resistance or changed circumstances. Let a concrete
situation reveal the relevant distinctions and judgments. Choose what naturally
characterizes this person; these are possibilities, not a checklist or a compulsory
reasoning sequence. Carry supported rhythm, humor, reserve, intensity and forms of
address into the prose without reducing the person to slogans or verbal tics.

First person should convey a way of thinking and relating to the reader. A capability
list prefixed with "I," generic expert virtues, a political label or a miniature
encyclopedia biography does not meet this requirement. Do not open with source counts,
extraction history or an explanation of Agent Skills. A natural invitation or question
can make the next exchange imaginable; do not force everyone into the same welcoming
speech, paragraph count or catchphrase.

Place one brief editorial note in the later **Scope and current status** section (or
its translated equivalent) identifying the introduction as a source-grounded persona
reconstruction, newly written rather than a historical quotation. Group the introduction's
editorial-basis and birth-date source links with that note. Keep both out of the opening
and the transition from the complete introduction to orientation/navigation.
First person does not authorize fabricated memories, credentials, quotations or claims
to literally be the person. Keep consequential scope limits beside the promises they
qualify. The rest of the README, maintainer records and this metatool's own documentation
remain ordinary documentation; they need not speak in character.
Move source counts, reading statistics, build history and assessment procedures to the
relevant later sections or linked records. Preserve an effective, supported opening;
do not rewrite it merely to make it livelier, friendlier or longer.

## Orient the reader and provide navigation

After the complete introduction, add one concise orientation sentence
about relationships or questions that commonly draw this persona's attention. Derive
it from the actual material; generic claims of rigorous, profound or multidimensional
analysis do not orient the reader. An arrow sequence is not required.

Provide concise links to the main sections in body order, including uses, getting
started, structure, sources and scope/status. Check the anchors and update them when
headings are translated or changed. Navigation must not interrupt the introduction.

## Give the reader a way in

### What to think about together

Describe uses through the situation a reader brings and the relationships or judgments
the perspective would examine. Topic names alone are not enough. Show several distinct
situations by default, adapting their number to available support rather than setting
a universal minimum. Do not repeat the opening's problem type in every item. Where
supported, include practical difficulties, conceptual disagreements, relationships,
aesthetics, everyday interests or other distinct contexts. Let expressive range emerge
through concerns and situations; do not make every persona a tutor, critic or comforter.

For example, a Graeber package might distinguish the meaning of work, care and refusal,
rules and recognition, and the value of play, if its retained material supports them.
That illustrates variety, not topics required for other personas or a claim that a
particular package was tested. Suggested questions can be tried directly without sample
answers. Keep use ideas within the actual supported scope and distinguish intended
applications from demonstrated effects.

### Getting started

Begin with the shortest steps readers must perform to install and invoke the skill in
the actual host. Use real repository URLs, frontmatter skill names and paths. State the
actual default language and how to request a change explicitly; a message in another
language alone does not override the default. The first action must be findable before
the reader understands maintenance directories, assessment protocols or context recovery.

Separate these first-use steps from manual system-prompt configuration and detailed
loading instructions, linking complex configuration to dedicated guidance where useful.
All setup routes still require the complete core, voice and frameworks by default,
with relevant topic/source modules on demand. Do not imply that the core alone supplies
the full runtime. State necessary operational requirements accurately at the appropriate
level of detail, including recovery instructions where the host requires them.

## Map repository and loading relationships

Provide a compact map or table explaining the core, voice, frameworks, topic modules
and maintenance records, with ordinary Markdown links to actual files. Include only
existing files, directories and discovery entries; adapt to the current layout without
requiring directory migration. A small repository may be clearer as a table.

Distinguish directory membership, default loading, on-demand loading and skill discovery.
If arrows are used, label what they mean: a discovery symlink is not a second runtime,
and directory membership is not a loading instruction. Maintenance and assessment records
stay outside ordinary runtime loading. A diagram does not replace clickable file links.

## Explain sources through their contributions

Organize sources by questions, concerns or reasoning responsibilities, explaining what
the materials help the reader understand. A source can support several topics and a
topic can connect to several sources. Retain titles, authors or coauthors, relevant
editions or periods, primary contributions and links to actual reference modules; layer
the presentation when needed instead of reducing it to a title inventory.

Grouping does not imply agreement. Preserve differences among periods, coauthors,
interview interlocutors and unresolved contradictions. Distinguish original source
positions, cross-source synthesis and contemporary applications. Relationship maps
cannot replace attribution. Detailed bibliographies may be collapsed or linked, but
important attribution and source limitations affecting use must remain visible.

## State reconstruction scope and current status

Keep the introduction's reconstruction notice and its editorial-basis and birth-date
source links together and visible in this later section, headed **Scope and current
status** or its translated equivalent. The notice identifies the opening as newly
written persona prose rather than a historical quotation; the links lead to the actual
supporting records. Do not repeat this notice beside the introduction or hide it solely
in a collapsed block. Source-contribution links remain with the sources they explain.

State supported periods, domains, source boundaries and the current version's actual
status. Explain what that means for use in ordinary language. Distinguish structural
checks (package organization and links), source-based editorial review (support and
attribution), diagnostic responses (specific observed outputs) and recognition assessment
(the applicable comparison protocol). None substitutes for the others.

If recognition has not run, say so; retain Candidate and its actual reasons. A historical
version's passing result does not certify the current version. Preserve the separate
scope of research outcomes. Editorial quality or layout improvements cannot confer
acceptance, recognizability, authenticity or model performance.

Keep consequential source/scope limitations beside the promises they qualify, including
near the opening when necessary. These are specific limits on a promise, not a reason
to move or duplicate the general reconstruction notice there. Use this section for
overall scope without repeating the same disclaimer everywhere. Do not hide essential
limits solely in collapsed blocks or substitute an unexplained experimental/status label.
Keep edition-dependent quotation limits beside quotation promises.

By default, reading-unit counts, exact word counts, model settings, call counts, build
history and assessment procedures belong in existing linked maintainer records, usually
`transworld-identity/`. Endpoint failures or exhausted budgets belong there too; the
README explains which assessment remains incomplete. Do not copy a completion report
into the opening. End with the actual license link and explain exclusions for original
sources and other separately governed material.

## Organize the page visually

Paragraphs convey voice and judgment; lists present possible uses; tables support mappings
and comparisons; diagrams explain relationships; links lead to detailed records. Group
installation, sources, scope and assessment information so they do not become consecutive
long passages. Vary paragraph length and create a visible hierarchy without deleting
substantive conditions just to shorten sections. Never collapse or split the complete
introduction to bring navigation higher.

Choose the clearest form separately for topic connections, source contributions and
loading relationships. Three Mermaid diagrams, or any diagram count, are not a universal
requirement or a substitute for layout quality. A reasoning diagram must not imply that
the persona always follows one process; use a relationship map or table for optional
concerns and connections. Use short labels, legible edges and a manageable number of
nodes, rather than one excessively wide diagram. Meaning must not depend on color alone.

Accompany diagrams with a brief prose explanation and an accessible description
(including alt text for images or an equivalent text account for Mermaid). Keep navigable
links outside diagrams as well. During production, inspect the GitHub view or a compatible
renderer at normal and narrow/mobile widths for wrapping, clipping, overlaps and text
legibility. If rendering was not checked, record that accurately; do not claim visual
verification from source inspection or link checks alone.

## Preserve and selectively recover

For an existing README, use this bounded revision sequence:

1. Read the README, core, voice, frameworks, scope and status records to establish which
   promises and characterizations remain supported.
2. Classify relevant passages as **preserve, relocate, qualify, rewrite or remove**.
   Prioritize effective openings, useful situations and unrelated user edits.
3. Reorganize existing content before filling gaps in navigation, uses and source
   contributions. Do not regenerate the whole page merely to fit the default structure.
   Move an opening reconstruction notice and its editorial-basis/birth-date source links
   together to **Scope and current status**, preserving accurate wording and link targets.
4. Condense maintenance narratives into links to existing records, retaining their
   consequences for use and the actual status.
5. Review prose, section responsibilities, file paths, anchors, diagrams and readability
   at normal and narrow widths. Apply the four editorial questions below.
6. Briefly record major decisions and only checks actually completed in existing editorial
   or migration/provenance notes; do not create another scoring system.

Keep effective prose through directory, metadata, status and evaluation-protocol changes;
edit substantive promises when their support or scope changes. Retain the existing README language
when it matches the Skill's declared default or an explicit user choice for the README;
otherwise align it during the requested README update, preserving meaning and scope.
When an introduction rewrite or an upgrade to this README standard is requested,
adapt an older third-person opening to the first-person and birth-date contract above,
with its reconstruction notice and supporting links in the later scope/status section.
A layout-only change preserves compliant opening prose; it does not authorize rewriting
immutable historical examples or migrating unrelated persona repositories.
This rule upgrade does not itself authorize batch changes to other persona repositories;
apply it within the requested README creation or maintenance task.

When earlier migrations flattened the prose, inspect available local history or
supplied earlier versions. Recover useful passages selectively against current evidence
and scope, retaining current installation and truthful status. Do not restore an old
README wholesale. If no earlier version is available, write from supported current
material; do not claim to have recovered the original voice.

## Keep attribution and existing examples identifiable

Excluding a conversation-excerpt component does not erase attribution duties for
historical quotations, existing illustrations or preserved evidence records. Do not
rewrite historical migration reviews or assessment outputs to fit the new page structure.
When editing a current README containing exchanges, retain useful questions or situation
descriptions without accompanying answers; keep any necessary output evidence in its
existing linked records with provenance intact.

- **Suggested user prompt:** label it as something the reader can try; no observed
  response is implied.
- **Editorial illustration:** label a newly written situation locally and briefly.
  It is neither a historical quotation nor a verified package output. Do not use
  quotation styling that silently attributes invented wording to the person.
- **Saved package output in linked records:** give actual provenance and version applicability. Do not
  silently edit it and call the result observed behavior; label adaptations separately.
- **Historical quotation:** provide defensible attribution and a source locator.
  If the available edition cannot support it, paraphrase with appropriate qualification
  or remove the quote.

One local label can establish the kind of example without disclaimers on every sentence.
See the [complete structural example and historical editorial reviews](readme-examples.md).
They illustrate responsibilities and decisions, not a literary style to force onto everyone.

## Finish in the existing authoring pass

Answer these four questions with concrete references to the draft in the existing
completion/provenance record:

1. **Particularity:** What makes this opening about this perspective? Would changing
   only the name and subject nouns make it fit almost anyone? Does the first-person
   voice express supported attention, judgments and interaction rather than generic
   expertise or surface mimicry? Do the orientation, choice of uses and source organization
   continue its distinctive concerns and conditions for judgment beyond the introduction?
2. **Encounter:** What can the reader imagine it doing with a real question, including
   any characteristic challenge, patience or friction? Do uses offer distinct supported
   situations instead of repeating the opening or listing only topic names, without
   simulated answers or assumed successful outcomes?
3. **Entry:** Is there an actionable starting question or situation and a clear path
   to using the package? Does the README's language match the Skill's declared default
   or the user's explicit README language choice? Is the complete self-introduction
   visible immediately below the title, with no reconstruction notice or provenance links
   interrupting it or the transition to orientation/navigation? Are default section
   responsibilities covered, navigation and file links valid and in body order, and the
   shortest setup steps clear before detailed configuration? Does visual hierarchy make the page readable, with
   purposeful tables/diagrams, accessible explanations and an honest rendering-check record?
4. **Honesty:** Do promises, examples, source limitations and current status agree
   with the available evidence? Is the parenthesized birth date supported at its stated
   precision, with no death date or lifespan range in the opening, and is the newly
   written introduction identified as a reconstruction in the later **Scope and current
   status** section, alongside working links to its editorial basis and birth-date source?
   Do source contributions retain authorship, periods, disagreements and the distinction
   between source positions, synthesis and application? Are material limits visible, default/on-demand
   loading accurate and historical results kept within their actual version scope?

Revise specific weak passages once within this editorial pass. These are judgments,
not numerical personality scores or human validation. Do not measure character through
uniform word/paragraph counts, humor frequency, warmth or compulsory disagreement;
inspect distinctive concerns, conditional judgments and the range of situations instead.
No judge agents, comparative
benchmarks, readability targets, adjective counts, mandatory warmth scores or automatic
rewrite loops. Style uncertainty does not trigger another evaluation cycle; material
false claims still need correction or removal.

After assessment, patch status and affected qualifications without regenerating the
README. If a README-only edit leaves all recognition inputs unchanged, preserve current
assessment applicability and make zero new recognition calls. If drafting reveals a
runtime defect, record it and use the existing scoped repair rules; do not silently
expand a README task into a persona rebuild. Mechanical tests can verify byte
preservation, links, hashes and call consumption, not literary quality or human presence.
